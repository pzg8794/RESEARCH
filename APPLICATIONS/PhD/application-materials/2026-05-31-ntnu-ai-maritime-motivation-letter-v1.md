# NTNU PhD Motivation Letter Draft (v1)

Position: PhD Candidate in AI-Enabled Maritime Systems for Environmental Monitoring (Job 301402)
Deadline: 31 May 2026

Dear Hiring Committee,

I am applying for the PhD position in AI-Enabled Maritime Systems for Environmental Monitoring at the Department of Ocean Operations and Civil Engineering, NTNU. The COD-SPAWN project's combination of autonomous maritime sensing, environmental intelligence, and applied scientific impact strongly matches my research interests and long-term goals.

My core motivation is to build trustworthy AI systems that can operate under real-world constraints and support decisions in complex environments. I am particularly interested in the challenge at the heart of this position: transforming multimodal observations from autonomous platforms into robust, interpretable environmental indicators for cod spawning and pollution dynamics. This is an area where technical rigor in machine learning, sensing integration, and systems validation can produce direct value for sustainable coastal management.

In prior research and project work, I have focused on data-driven modeling, algorithmic design, and reproducible experimentation across interdisciplinary problem settings. I am comfortable working from problem definition through implementation and evaluation, and I value research that bridges methodological novelty with practical deployment realities. I am especially motivated by interdisciplinary collaboration, and this role's integration with marine biology, environmental monitoring, and ocean engineering is exactly the kind of research environment where I can contribute and grow.

At NTNU, I would aim to contribute to the development of robust AI and computer-vision pipelines for environmental monitoring on USVs, with a focus on performance, uncertainty awareness, and operational reliability. I am also strongly motivated to contribute to publications, collaborative field-focused research, and the wider research-group activities in Ocean and Coast initiatives.

Beyond the technical fit, I am drawn to NTNU's international and mission-driven research culture, and to the opportunity to work in Alesund in close connection with marine research infrastructure and partners. The position represents an ideal next step for my doctoral training and for building a long-term research profile at the intersection of AI, autonomous systems, and environmental sustainability.

Thank you for your consideration. I would be honored to contribute to COD-SPAWN and to NTNU's broader research mission.

Sincerely,

Piter Garcia
