## Working title
Responsibility and Traceability in AI-Supported Decision-Making: Governance of Model and Simulation Systems in Public Sector Practice

## 1) Background and rationale
AI-supported decision-making in public-sector contexts is increasingly mediated by model-based and simulation-based systems that are continuously updated and operationally embedded. In these environments, decisions are not purely automated or purely human, but produced through socio-technical arrangements where assumptions, uncertainty, model outputs, and institutional constraints interact. Existing governance frameworks often describe high-level principles (fairness, accountability, transparency) but provide limited empirical understanding of how responsibility and intervention are actually organized in day-to-day practice.

## 2) Aim
The project aims to examine how responsibility, traceability, and human oversight are enacted in AI-supported decision-making processes in public-sector organizations, with particular attention to exceptions, model errors, and manual overrides.

## 3) Research questions
1. How are responsibilities distributed and renegotiated when decisions are shaped by continuously updated models or simulations?
2. How are uncertainty, model updates, and failures documented, interpreted, and managed in operational use?
3. Under what conditions do organizations intervene manually, override model outputs, or apply fallback logic, and what are the governance implications?

## 4) Theoretical framing
The project will combine socio-technical and organizational perspectives from information systems and governance research, with concepts centered on accountability, traceability, and legitimacy in digitally mediated decision processes.

## 5) Data and empirical setting
Empirical work will focus on public-sector decision environments where computational models/simulations are used in operational practice. Data sources may include:
- Semi-structured interviews with key stakeholders
- Process and policy documentation
- Workflow artifacts and exception-handling records
- Observation of decision workflows (when feasible)

## 6) Methodology
A qualitative, practice-oriented design will be used:
- Case-based inquiry in selected organizational contexts
- Thematic analysis of interview and document material
- Process-oriented analysis of intervention and failure episodes

Where relevant to the research questions, the dissertation may include design or evaluation of practical artifacts (for example, traceability templates, exception-documentation structures, or governance-oriented documentation principles).

## 7) Expected contribution
Academic contributions:
- Empirical insight into how accountability is enacted in AI-supported operational decisions
- Conceptual clarification of traceability and responsibility under model uncertainty and change

Practical contributions:
- Actionable governance principles for exception handling and oversight
- Documentation and traceability support practices for public-sector AI use

## 8) Preliminary timeline (4-year format)
Year 1:
- Literature review, framing, protocol design, ethics preparation
- Scoping interviews and case selection

Year 2:
- Main empirical data collection
- Initial analysis and first article/manuscript draft

Year 3:
- Comparative analysis across cases
- Artifact design/evaluation (if included)
- Additional manuscripts

Year 4:
- Synthesis, dissertation completion, defense preparation

## 9) Feasibility note
The project is designed for iterative refinement in dialogue with supervisors and ongoing departmental research, with flexibility to sharpen empirical scope after initial case access and field mapping.
