# Diversity Statement Draft — UW Information Science

Status: **DRAFT — NOT SUBMITTED**
Official limit: **350 words**

I approach inclusion as a design and evaluation responsibility. It should shape how information is presented, how people participate, and how technical systems are tested—not be added after a system has already been built.

My preparation combines computer science, data science, K–12 teaching, and graduate study in disability and inclusive practices. In Rochester-area teaching placements, I have learned to design instruction that gives students multiple ways to access content, communicate, and demonstrate knowledge while preserving the rigor of the learning goal. That work has made me more attentive to the barriers created by unclear expectations, inaccessible materials, and systems that mistake one way of learning or communicating for ability itself.

I bring the same discipline to research. When I evaluate a learning or decision system, I do not want one aggregate result to hide who is consistently underserved. I ask what evidence is missing, which groups experience different errors or delays, whether the result is understandable and usable, and what human review remains necessary. My data-science work gives me tools to measure these questions; my teaching preparation keeps the people affected by them visible.

At UW, I would contribute through accessible research communication, inclusive teaching and mentoring, careful documentation, and research practices that examine subgroup and accessibility outcomes from the beginning. I can also help bridge technical and nontechnical communities by translating computational questions into language that allows more people to participate in reviewing the assumptions and consequences of a system.

The environment in which I do my best work has transparent expectations, accessible materials, structured mentoring, timely feedback, and respect for different ways of learning and communicating. I would help build that environment for others while continuing to learn from colleagues whose experiences and disciplines differ from my own.

For me, inclusion is not a separate service activity. It is part of producing better research, better education, and more trustworthy information systems.
