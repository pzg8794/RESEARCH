# Research Statement Plan — UW Information Science

Official length: **1,000–2,000 words**

## Purpose

Show a computational researcher studying reliable and equitable decisions under incomplete evidence, and explain why information practices, institutions, and affected people are part of technical validity.

## Core claims

1. High-stakes AI often operates with incomplete, delayed, noisy, or uneven evidence; aggregate accuracy can hide who is repeatedly missed.
2. Piter brings a computational/data-science foundation in machine learning, bandit decision policies, simulation, reproducible evaluation, and disparity measurement.
3. The current thesis supplies a testable foundation: compare decision policies on utility, reliability, latency, and group disparities under controlled evidence degradation.
4. Human and institutional context changes what evidence exists and whether a technically strong output is usable, accessible, or harmful.
5. UW uniquely supports the combined study of AI reliability, responsible evaluation, health information, and accessibility.

## Candidate projects

### Project A — Evidence-aware clinical information support

Measure when retrieval/model pipelines become unreliable for different populations as clinical or scientific evidence becomes incomplete or outdated. Compare calibration, utility, abstention/clarification behavior, and disparity across evidence conditions.

### Project B — Accessible evidence and decision quality

Test whether document accessibility and evidence presentation change the reliability of downstream human-AI decisions, especially for people using assistive technologies. Keep this project only if the faculty-paper review supports a feasible computational contribution.

## Existing evidence to draw from

- Fairness-aware bandit thesis and shared simulation/evaluation framework.
- Quantum routing work as evidence of sequential decisions under uncertainty, not as a second application domain to explain at length.
- Bioinformatics/clinical-equity preparation indexed in the common source map.
- Inclusive CS teaching and disability-access work as evidence of stakeholder-aware evaluation.

## Avoid or reduce

- Do not list every Puzzle Plan domain.
- Do not present EQUITAS as a finished validated method.
- Do not imply clinical deployment or patient-data results that do not exist.
- Do not turn the statement into a biography; personal context belongs in the 350-word statement.
- Do not claim a faculty member will advise the work or is a collaborator.
