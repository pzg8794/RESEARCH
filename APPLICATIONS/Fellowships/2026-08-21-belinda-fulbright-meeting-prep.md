## Madrid

### What to remember

**English → civic participation + academic subjects.**

### Talking point

> Madrid stood out to me because English is not necessarily isolated to an English class. I looked at the bilingual-school system and especially Global Classrooms, where students use English for Model UN-style civic debate. I liked that because language becomes a tool for students to communicate ideas and participate, rather than just something they are tested on.

### Concrete detail

Comunidad de Madrid reports that Global Classrooms expanded to **166 public bilingual schools**, involving **1,660 students in 3º ESO**, using English in Model UN-style debate and simulation.

Official source:  
https://www.comunidad.madrid/noticias/2026/02/24/comunidad-madrid-duplica-centros-publicos-alumnos-participan-programa-debate-escolar-global-classrooms

### Inclusion connection

> I also looked at Plan IncluYO. What caught my attention was the focus on identifying barriers to learning and participation in the student’s context rather than treating every difference as something inside the student that needs fixing.

Official source:  
https://www.comunidad.madrid/educacion/atencion-diferencias-individuales-alumnado

---

## Galicia

### What to remember

**English → multilingualism, not replacement of identity.**

### Talking point

> Galicia interested me because it is already a multilingual environment. I looked at the plurilingual school network, where English can be used to teach other subjects, so students are not learning English completely separately from content.

### Concrete detail

The Xunta continues its **Red de centros plurilingües** across primary and secondary education, with related Plurinfantil and Pluribach programs. Current authorized schools include examples of non-language subjects such as Visual Arts and Physical Education taught in English.

Official sources:  
https://www.xunta.gal/dog/Publicados/2026/20260424/AnuncioG0761-170426-0001_es.html  
https://www.xunta.gal/dog/Publicados/2025/20251014/AnuncioG0761-061025-0001_es.html

### Why it interests me

> What I like about Galicia is that English is being added in a place where regional language and identity already matter. That makes me interested in how students can gain another language without treating their existing language or identity as something they need to leave behind.

---

## La Rioja

### What to remember

**English → practical/professional application.**

### Talking point

> La Rioja stood out differently because of the vocational side. I looked at their bilingual vocational education, where English can be integrated into professional modules. I like the idea of students using English for something connected to what they may actually do professionally.

### Concrete detail

In bilingual vocational programs, English can be integrated into at least **two professional modules in both the first and second year**.

Official source:  
https://larioja.org/educarioja-fp/fr/nueva-portada-formacion-profesional/accesos-directos/puedo-estudiar/fp-dual

There are also official examples involving technical modules such as databases, web applications, programming, and server-side web development.

### Inclusion connection

La Rioja also maintains specific support pathways related to autism, homebound education, and late-arriving students with significant language needs.

Official source:  
https://larioja.org/edu-aten-diversidad/en/procedimientos-electronicos
