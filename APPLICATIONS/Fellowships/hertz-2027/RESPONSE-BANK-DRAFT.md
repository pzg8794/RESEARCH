## Technical identity

I build and evaluate computational systems that must make decisions when evidence is incomplete, delayed, noisy, or uneven. My work combines machine learning, sequential decision algorithms, software and data systems, and reproducible evaluation. The central question is not only whether an algorithm performs well on average, but when it fails, what evidence is missing, and who or what repeatedly receives worse outcomes.

## Most significant current research

My current research develops a shared experimental framework for fairness-aware sequential resource allocation. It compares multi-armed, contextual, adversarial, and neural bandit methods in quantum-network routing and a distinct clinical decision simulation. The shared technical structure is an agent that selects among possible actions, receives partial feedback, and adapts future choices. I evaluate utility, latency, reliability, robustness, and group- or flow-level disparities under controlled missing, delayed, noisy, and uneven context.

The hard part is not placing two domains in one document. It is designing a reusable computational interface while preserving the assumptions and consequences that do not transfer. I have built Python experiment, validation, logging, dataset, and comparison workflows that make each claim traceable to a configuration and result. The next stage is to strengthen the mathematical and empirical basis for when fairness-aware adjustments improve outcomes without hiding tradeoffs.

## Creative problem-solving example

I often begin with the smallest stable part of a problem that can be measured. I first map the current process and data, establish a baseline, implement one bounded change, test it, and compare the result with the prior system. This approach shaped both my research framework and my software-engineering work. It avoids automating a process simply because it is repetitive and avoids using AI where a rule, validation step, or conventional workflow is more reliable.

## Greater-good direction

High-stakes systems often operate on evidence that is incomplete for structural rather than random reasons. Some people, communities, or institutions are less represented in data, experience longer delays, or communicate information in forms a system was not designed to accept. I want to develop methods that expose those conditions, measure their effect on decisions, and help systems seek more evidence, defer, or route cases to human review when confidence is not justified.

## Leadership and collaboration

My leadership style is to make complex work understandable and traceable. In research, I organize assumptions, configurations, experiments, validation, and reporting so collaborators can reproduce what happened and challenge the interpretation. In software work, I connect implementation with documentation and operational use. In K–12 computer-science teaching preparation, I design multiple ways for learners to access content and demonstrate knowledge without lowering the technical goal. Across these settings, I help teams move from a broad idea to smaller pieces that can be built, reviewed, and improved.

## Academic growth sought

Doctoral study will allow me to deepen the mathematical foundations of sequential decision-making, uncertainty, and fairness-aware evaluation; develop stronger experimental methods; and learn how to work responsibly with health and scientific information. I want to turn a cross-domain framework into defensible research contributions rather than treat the current concepts as finished methods.
