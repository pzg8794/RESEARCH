### Experience

My experience combines data systems, program evaluation, inclusive education,
disability access, and technical communication. I am completing graduate
degrees in Data Science at Rochester Institute of Technology and Teaching
Computer Science K-12 at the University of Rochester, along with an Advanced
Certificate in Leadership in Disability and Inclusive Practices. As an NSF
Robert Noyce Scholar and computer science teacher candidate, I design learning
experiences in coding, robotics, digital fluency, and responsible AI for
students with varied strengths and needs. I use Universal Design for Learning
and multiple participation routes to expand access without lowering
expectations.

My professional and research work also prepares me for Mosaic's monitoring,
evaluation, documentation, and communication responsibilities. At VEDADATA, I
designed data-ingestion, validation, and analytics workflows and collaborated
with technical and nontechnical colleagues to clarify requirements and improve
reliability. At VIOME, I developed healthcare-oriented machine-learning
workflows involving preprocessing, feature engineering, evaluation, and
reporting. In my current RIT graduate assistantship, I build reproducible
evaluation systems with structured data collection, automated validation,
experiment tracking, and comparison-ready reporting.

Across these roles, I have learned to listen before proposing a technical
solution, document assumptions, make evidence visible, and communicate results
in forms that different collaborators can use. My disability and inclusion
work is organized through the Puzzle Plan, an interdisciplinary framework
connecting lived experience, education, data, and research, and EQUITAS, its
equity-focused lens. These experiences would help me support local staff with
program monitoring, data quality, planning, training materials, and
communication while remaining accountable to local expertise and priorities.

Personal international experience not already documented should be added by
Piter here before submission.

### Motivation to Apply

I am applying because the Carman Fellowship brings together the work I want to
do long term: strengthening community-based services for people with
disabilities, building practical monitoring and evaluation systems, supporting
inclusive education, and helping organizations turn evidence into sustainable
decisions. Mosaic's accompaniment model is especially important to me. I do
not want to arrive with a prefabricated solution. I want to listen to local
staff, families, and partner leaders; understand the systems they have already
built; and contribute tools that they judge useful.

I could bring practical strengths in data collection, quality checks,
reproducible evaluation, technical documentation, inclusive teaching, and
cross-functional communication. I can help organize program information,
identify gaps or inconsistencies, build clear monitoring artifacts, develop
accessible training and reporting materials, and connect day-to-day evidence
with planning and sustainability. My Spanish and English fluency also give me
experience moving between languages and communication contexts, while I would
approach Kiswahili as a serious learning responsibility rather than assuming
English will be sufficient.

I expect the fellowship to challenge my assumptions and deepen my
understanding of disability services, community ownership, and international
program development. My personal goal is to become a more accountable
researcher and educator: someone who can build rigorous systems without
separating technical quality from dignity, culture, and access. After the
fellowship, I would carry those lessons into research, inclusive CS education,
and responsible AI work that supports communities rather than merely studying
them.

### Career Goals

My long-term goal is to work at the intersection of responsible AI, data
science, inclusive computer science education, and disability justice. I want
to design and evaluate systems that help educators, health and social-service
organizations, and community partners make better decisions while preserving
the dignity and agency of the people represented in their data.

The Carman Fellowship would provide practical experience that graduate study
alone cannot: working within a community-based disability organization,
supporting local staff across program areas, adapting monitoring and evaluation
tools to real constraints, and learning how international partnerships can
build capacity without displacing local leadership. That experience would
strengthen my future work as an educator, applied researcher, and program
designer. I would use it to develop more grounded approaches to inclusive
technology, evaluation, and professional learning, and to build lasting
partnerships between technical, educational, and disability-service
communities.

### Communication Skills

Maximum: 250 words.

I am fluent in English and a native/fluent Spanish speaker. My education and
professional work have required me to translate complex technical information
for people with different roles, levels of expertise, and communication needs.
As a data practitioner, I have documented requirements, validation findings,
and analytical results for technical and nontechnical collaborators. As a
computer science teacher candidate, I use multiple forms of explanation,
visual support, modeling, and feedback so that people can participate without
being limited to one communication mode.

I would bring the same care to communication between Mosaic's U.S.-based and
Tanzania-based partners. I would confirm decisions in writing, distinguish
facts from assumptions, keep action items and owners visible, and ask for
clarification rather than pretending to understand. I also recognize that
English proficiency does not replace cultural or local-language learning. If
selected, I would begin Kiswahili study before arrival and continue learning
from local colleagues so that I can communicate more respectfully and reduce
dependence on others for routine interactions.
