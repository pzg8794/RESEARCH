## Why these leads strengthen the supplementary-project idea

The project's strongest framing is not "I will study Spanish teachers" or "I will bring my framework to Spain." It is:

> I want to learn how educators and local systems identify and remove barriers before students are treated as if they are failing, especially where neurodivergence, disability, language, culture, health, and identity intersect.

These scholars are useful because their current work already touches pieces of that question:

- **barriers to inclusion and autism** — USC / Galicia
- **multicultural + inclusive education and teacher practice** — UDC / Galicia
- **diversity, adaptive pedagogy, and barrier removal** — UCM / Madrid
- **special-education teacher preparation** — UCM / Madrid
- **inclusion/equity, UDL, ADHD, ASD, high ability, homebound education, and regional teacher learning** — Universidad de La Rioja

## Outreach strategy

Do not ask for a formal commitment first.

A first message should ask for a **brief informational conversation** or for a recommendation of a local educator/researcher/resource to learn from. The request should make clear that:

- this is for a Fulbright Spain ETA application;
- the teaching-assistant role is primary;
- the supplementary project is modest and exploratory;
- the goal is to learn from existing Spanish practice rather than impose a U.S. model;
- no research access, data, affiliation, or supervision is being presumed.

Possible first question:

> I am especially interested in how schools identify and respond to barriers to participation before students are framed as failing. From your work in the region, what people, programs, or public resources would you recommend I study to understand that more responsibly?
