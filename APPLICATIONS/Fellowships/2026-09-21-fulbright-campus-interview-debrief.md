## Executive summary

The committee did **not** signal that the application needs a new direction. The strongest direct feedback was to make the existing application **more concrete, more personal, and more visibly connected to life in Spain beyond the classroom**.

The application was described as already **“nicely focused”** on teaching. The panel also said the interview gave them a **better sense of Piter** than the written materials did. 5. Use Piter's existing personal and family connections to Spain to demonstrate authentic familiarity and curiosity.

## 1.2 What Piter would represent about the United States

**INTERVIEW EVIDENCE — ~01:06:51-01:10:21**

The committee asked how Piter's experience moving to the United States as a young adult would shape his role as a Fulbright cultural ambassador.

Piter centered his answer on **advocacy**.

He explained that his coursework and reflection at the University of Rochester helped him find language for lessons he had learned in the United States, especially:

- knowing how and when to ask for help;
- finding the right information and resources;
- communicating with teachers, advisers, and supervisors;
- being honest and direct while also preparing in advance;
- advocating for oneself instead of silently assuming that asking for support will cause negative consequences.

He connected this to an earlier period when, as an immigrant/student, he had been afraid that disclosing difficulties might jeopardize his education or scholarship.

### Core ambassadorial idea that emerged

Piter's U.S. story is not simply “the United States gave me opportunities.” It is more nuanced:

> One important thing he learned in the United States is how access to information, communication, institutional resources, and self-advocacy can change what a person is able to do.

That is a stronger and more distinctive cultural-exchange theme than a generic positive description of the United States.

## 1.3 Inclusive teaching, multimodality, and language learners

**INTERVIEW EVIDENCE — ~01:11:07-01:15:34**

The panel asked about teaching approaches, challenges, and how Piter would translate his practices for language learners.

Piter discussed a technology-supported lesson structure in which students could encounter the same content through combinations of:

- video,
- text,
- audio,
- images or figures.

He also identified a real limitation: providing many options can itself become distracting. His answer was that effective differentiation requires learning who the students are and deciding which supports are useful for which learners rather than simply giving everyone every option.

When the panel asked how this would translate to language learners, Piter connected the answer to his own English-learning experience and emphasized allowing learners different ways to take in information and demonstrate understanding.

### What the panel was testing

The committee was not only asking whether Piter knows inclusive-teaching terminology. They were asking whether he can explain what those practices **look like with actual learners**.

This becomes one of the clearest written-application revision points in the later feedback section.

# 7. Interview-answer lessons for future high-stakes conversations

These are **not committee directives**. They are source-grounded observations from the transcript that may improve future interviews.

## 7.1 Lead with the answer, then give the story

Several answers became long before reaching the central point.

A stronger structure is:

> direct answer -> one example -> why it matters to Fulbright.

This preserves Piter's depth without making the panel search for the conclusion.

## 7.2 Separate “I do not watch TV news” from “I do not follow current affairs”

The panel reacted to Piter saying he avoids politics/news until he clarified the distinction.

For future interviews, say the precise thing immediately:

> “I don't rely much on television news; I follow issues through other sources.”

Then identify those sources.

## 7.3 Personal relationships are strongest when paired with current evidence

Family and community conversations make the application authentic. They become more persuasive when Piter can pair them with current, independent sources.

## 7.4 Keep technical explanations tied to human consequences

The AI/framework answer became strongest when it returned to:

- students;
- age appropriateness;
- safeguards;
- fairness;
- control.

That is the useful bridge between Piter's technical background and the ETA role.
