## What is SaNDAI?

**One sentence:** SaNDAI Global is a consulting and technology company that helps organizations—especially nonprofits, public agencies, healthcare, education, and community organizations—use their existing data more effectively through business intelligence, dashboards, responsible AI automation, and staff capacity building.

In plain language, SaNDAI helps clients:

- clean, connect, and understand scattered data;
- build dashboards and reports for better decisions;
- automate practical tasks such as reminders, summaries, and follow-ups;
- use AI with human review, privacy, and accountability;
- train staff so the organization can actually use the solution.

Their central idea is **“AI starts with BI.”** In other words: understand and organize the data first, then add intelligent automation where it is useful. That is closely aligned with your own approach.

## Why am I a strong fit?

### Your four strongest points

1. **You bridge business and technology.** You do not begin with “use AI.” You first understand the process, people, data, risks, and success criteria.
2. **You have real technical depth.** Python, SQL, analytics, ML, data pipelines, experimentation, validation, and reporting.
3. **You think responsibly.** You separate recommendations from authorized action and preserve human review for consequential decisions.
4. **You can explain complex ideas.** Your teaching work strengthens your ability to communicate with clients, teams, and nontechnical stakeholders.

### Your core philosophy

> I first understand the business process and the data, establish a baseline, and improve one measurable part at a time. I use the simplest reliable technology—ordinary automation when that is sufficient, and AI only where it adds real value. Then I test, measure, revise, and expand only when the evidence supports it.

### Best concrete example: MLADIS

> In MLADIS, I organized raw information into increasingly reliable and useful layers. I kept source-backed facts separate from generated suggestions, placed human authorization outside the recommendation system, and used independent verification rather than calling an entire workflow complete because one component worked. That made the system useful without allowing AI output to silently become an authorized action.

## Likely questions and short answers

### Why SaNDAI?

> SaNDAI works where data, business needs, responsible AI, and community impact meet. That fits both my technical background and how I approach improvement: understand the real workflow first, build something practical, measure it, and make sure people can actually use it.

### What can you contribute immediately?

> I can help investigate a client's current process, structure requirements, clean and analyze data, build Python or SQL workflows, validate outputs, document decisions, and translate technical findings into clear recommendations. I can also identify when ordinary automation is more appropriate than AI.

### How do you handle an unclear client problem?

> I separate facts from assumptions, identify the desired outcome and stakeholders, map the current process, inspect the available data, and establish a baseline. Then I propose one small, measurable, reversible improvement rather than prematurely redesigning everything.

### How do you use AI responsibly?

> I treat AI as one tool inside a larger system. I verify the data and workflow, protect sensitive information, preserve human review, test outputs against a baseline, and keep consequential action behind an explicit authorization boundary.

### What if automation fails?

> First contain the problem. Then determine which layer failed—source data, transformation, retrieval, model, workflow, or authorization. Correct it, validate the correction, and resume only when the evidence supports doing so.

### Are you available for at least 20 hours per month?

> Yes. I can commit at least 20 hours per month and coordinate the exact schedule around my established teaching and academic obligations.

### What is a weakness or growth area?

> I naturally see many possible improvements in a system. I manage that by defining the immediate business outcome, prioritizing one bounded deliverable, and using evidence and checkpoints to prevent scope from expanding unnecessarily.
