# SaNDAI CEO Interview Rapid Review

## Three messages to land

1. **I understand the business and the data before choosing technology.**
2. **I improve one measurable, reversible part at a time.**
3. **I communicate what is known, what is inferred, and what still needs verification.**

## Thirty second introduction

> I am a data scientist, AI researcher, educator, and longtime systems engineer. I am strongest at turning an unclear business problem into a reliable workflow, analysis, or recommendation that technical and nontechnical people can use. I am interested in SaNDAI because the work combines real client responsibility with data, automation, AI, and community impact.

## My core method

**Understand the business → understand the data → establish a baseline → choose the simplest reliable improvement → build one bounded feature → test with users → measure → revise → scale only if justified**

**Important:** Automation is broader than AI. A rule, form, script, API, integration, or dashboard may be the right answer.

## Assessment in one minute

- Immediate risk: uncontrolled use of organizational information and unverified service answers.
- Best first improvement: acknowledge and route one website inquiry form.
- Why: measurable, useful, maintainable, and possible with ordinary automation.
- Privacy decision: executive authorization does not make every field or external tool appropriate.
- Crisis rule: stop harm first; contained, changed, and resolved are different states.

## Best evidence stories

- **MLADIS:** source evidence → validated information → bounded use; human authorization remains separate; stale approval and uncertain sends are blocked.
- **Quantum and AI research:** Python validation, structured logging, experiment tracking, and reproducible comparison of large outputs.
- **Healthcare ML:** preprocessing, feature engineering, model experimentation, evaluation, and careful claim boundaries.
- **Teaching:** explain complex work in plain language, check understanding, and adapt without losing accuracy.

## Strong phrases

- “The business goal should determine the technology.”
- “I would compare the improvement with an agreed baseline.”
- “I separate facts from assumptions before recommending action.”
- “I would offer a smaller permitted step that still moves the client toward the goal.”
- “A faster process is not better if it produces unreliable answers or moves work somewhere else.”
- “Contained is not the same as resolved.”
