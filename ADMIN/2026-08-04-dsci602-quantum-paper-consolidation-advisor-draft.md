Dear Travis and Dan,

I would like to ask whether we can formalize my DSCI 602 thesis around the
revision and extension of our quantum-routing manuscript while preserving the

this would consolidate the remaining work rather than begin a different
project.

The ICNP submission was not accepted, but the reviews provide a useful and
manageable roadmap. Reviewer A, an expert reviewer, gave the paper a Weak
Accept and specifically validated the evaluation design, cross-testbed
evidence, and capacity-paradox finding. Reviewer C also recognized the
matched-evaluation methodology as a real contribution and provided a concrete
revision checklist. My goal is to preserve A's accepted core and make the
concrete changes needed to convert C, giving the revised paper support from two
of the three review perspectives. My proposed order is to clarify those
strengths first by adding the joint decision-loop pseudocode, complete context
and hyperparameter specifications, and an explicit allocator-policy interface.
The next stages would physically ground the threat regimes and add the
medium-scale evaluation and large-topology diagnosis requested in the reviews.
Reviewer B's concerns would remain a secondary risk audit throughout this
process.

Under this plan, the quantum manuscript revision would be the primary
technical implementation, and the clinical testbed would remain the fairness
and transfer comparison. The shared outputs would be a reproducible bandit
interface, context specification, fairness-aware evaluation framework, and
cross-domain analysis. Any required separation among assistantship work,
coauthored research, course credit, and thesis milestones would be documented
explicitly.

This focused scope would make my final semester more sustainable while I
complete both degrees and maintain the paid work I need. Could you please let
me know whether this consolidation is academically appropriate for DSCI 602,
what revised scope or approval documentation you would want, and whether you
would prefer to review a one-page plan before we meet?

Thank you,

Piter Garcia
