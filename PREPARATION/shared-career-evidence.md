## Research Direction In Plain Language

My central research question is how an AI system should make fair and reliable
decisions when it has limited resources and receives incomplete, delayed,
noisy, or uneven feedback. I compare algorithms that repeatedly choose an
action, observe the result, and improve future choices. I test the same core
logic in clinical simulations, where diagnostic attention is limited, and in
quantum-network simulations, where routing and computational resources are
limited. I measure useful performance, latency, reliability, robustness, and
differences in outcomes across groups or network flows.

## Puzzle Plan And EQUITAS

The **Puzzle Plan** is Piter Garcia's interdisciplinary planning framework. It
connects engineering, artificial intelligence, bioinformatics, disability and
neurodivergence, lived experience, advocacy, and computer science education.
The pieces are not separate career changes; they support one goal: building
technical systems and learning environments that recognize the evidence people
bring and provide support before they are dismissed or excluded.

**EQUITAS**, or Equity-Quantified Integrated Trauma-Aware Systems, is the
fairness-aware evaluation framework within that agenda. It asks who may be
missed when evidence is incomplete or uneven and evaluates more than aggregate
accuracy. Depending on the setting, it considers utility, reliability,
latency, robustness, access, and group-level differences. The framework guides
questions and evaluation; it is not a claim that one formula solves every
domain.
