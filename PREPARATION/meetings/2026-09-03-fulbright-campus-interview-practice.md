# Fulbright Spain ETA Campus Interview — Quick Practice Sheet

Updated: 2026-09-03

Purpose: turn the Spanish evaluator's feedback into short, reusable interview
answers. These are speaking anchors, not scripts to memorize.

## 30-second anchor

I am a Dominican American computer scientist and teacher preparing to teach
English in Spain. Learning English changed how I could make my knowledge and
identities visible and advocate for myself. I want to help students use English
without surrendering identity or voice, using inclusive teaching and
technology as supports for human communication—not replacements for it.

## Likely questions and answer anchors

### Why Fulbright ETA and why Spain?

- English changed what I could express, advocate for, and contribute after
  moving from the Dominican Republic to New York.
- Spain lets me learn from another multilingual educational system while
  contributing classroom experience in CS, science, and inclusive education.
- I am applying for reciprocal exchange: teach, listen, adapt, and bring useful
  practices back to high-need schools and teacher-preparation communities.

### Why use computer science or AI in an English classroom?

- The purpose remains English communication.
- Technology provides low-stakes rehearsal, multiple ways to respond, and a
  concrete topic students can question together.
- School-approved AI is a rehearsal partner, never a substitute for teachers,
  peers, or cultural exchange.

### Give one example of inclusive teaching

Use this four-part structure: **barrier -> adaptation -> response -> lesson**.

- Barrier: language load, unfamiliar interfaces, executive-function demands,
  or fear of error can hide what a learner knows.
- Adaptation: clear goals, modeled steps, visual/hands-on/verbal entry points,
  partner roles, and varied participation formats.
- Response: observe participation and revise grouping, directions, or pace.
- Lesson: access should be designed before failure, without lowering the goal.

### How will you adapt in Spain?

- Spanish fluency helps me communicate; it does not mean I already understand
  the school or regional culture.
- Begin by listening, observing, documenting patterns, asking specific
  questions, and co-planning with the host teacher.
- Treat an activity that does not generate conversation as feedback and adjust.

### What will you contribute as a cultural ambassador?

- Share one Dominican American experience, not a claim to represent every
  American or Dominican experience.
- Discuss how migration, language, education, technology, and disability
  advocacy shaped my perspective.
- Invite regional perspectives and approach Galician language and culture with
  curiosity and respect if placed there.

### What is the Puzzle Plan project?

- A bounded, host-approved inquiry into designing access before failure where
  neurodivergence, chronic illness, language, culture, and identity intersect.
- Learn through public documents and conversations with willing adult
  educators—not through unauthorized research on students.
- If invited, co-create a bilingual, low-cost access map that local and
  Rochester educators can adapt.

### How will Fulbright create a multiplier effect?

- Return to teach computer science in high-need schools and pursue doctoral
  research connecting fairness, AI, education, and neurodivergent learners.
- Convert learning into reusable lessons, a practitioner brief, and workshops.
- Share both successful practices and limitations, invite Spanish collaborator
  feedback, and mentor future applicants.

### What current event are you following?

Use: **verified event -> why it matters locally -> educational connection ->
question you are still considering**. Use a current Spain-based source before
the interview; do not generalize from one story or claim expertise you do not
have.

## Evaluator-derived practice priorities

1. Keep technology subordinate to English learning and human exchange.
2. Give one concrete teaching example rather than a list of principles.
3. Connect a current event to education carefully and without overgeneralizing.
4. Explain community engagement as listening and sustained participation.
5. Practice with a multidisciplinary committee and use feedback to tighten the
   structure of each answer.

## Five-minute drill

1. Deliver the 30-second anchor once.
2. Answer any two questions above in 45-60 seconds each.
3. Check: Did I answer the question first? Did I give one example? Did I keep
   ETA/English central? Did I show listening and reciprocity?
4. Repeat only the weaker answer, cutting one unnecessary sentence.
