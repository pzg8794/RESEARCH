## Thesis In Plain Language

My thesis studies how limited resources can be allocated fairly when a system
must learn from incomplete feedback. I compare bandit algorithms, which choose
among possible actions, observe the selected result, and update future choices.
I use a shared evaluation framework in two settings: routing scarce resources
in quantum networks and allocating diagnostic attention in clinical
simulations. I test whether useful context improves decisions and whether a
fairness-aware decision step can reduce differences across patient groups or
network flows without sacrificing too much overall performance. I also test
what happens when context is missing, delayed, noisy, or systematically weaker
for particular groups or flows.

## Connection To The Larger Agenda

The thesis is one technical component of the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS research
agenda. The Puzzle Plan links engineering, AI, bioinformatics, disability and
neurodivergence, lived experience, advocacy, and CS education. EQUITAS is the
fairness-aware evaluation framework that asks who may be missed when evidence
is incomplete or uneven. The thesis tests which fairness principles transfer
between clinical and quantum systems and which must remain specific to the
people, risks, and resources involved.
